01Where it began

Manchester United started life in 1878 as Newton Heath LYR, a works team formed by men of the Lancashire and Yorkshire Railway. They played in green and gold on a rough pitch beside the carriage works.

By 1902 the club was nearly bankrupt. A group of local businessmen led by brewer John Henry Davies stepped in to save it, and the club took a new name: Manchester United. The shirts turned red soon after, and they have stayed red ever since.

02Theatre of Dreams

Old Trafford has been home since 1910. It was bombed in 1941 and rebuilt after the war. Today it holds around 74,000 people, which makes it the largest club stadium in England.

Sir Bobby Charlton called it the Theatre of Dreams, and the name stuck.

03Why the devil

In the 1960s Sir Matt Busby took the nickname Red Devils from Salford's rugby league side, which had earned it on a tour of France. He wanted a name with more bite than "the Busby Babes," a name that carried painful memories after Munich.

It fit. The club became fearless, attacking and hard to kill.

04The red half

Manchester has two colours. The derby with City is more than a century old, and it splits streets, pubs and families across the city.

This page takes a side. Manchester is Red.
